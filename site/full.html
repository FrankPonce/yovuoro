04Static sleep rules vs Yövahti
Same antenna, same forecasts. A different brain.
Static rulesfixed load thresholds, the industry rule of thumb
a threshold dial
  1. Sleeps when the cell is quieter than a fixed threshold.
  2. Same threshold every night, whatever is coming.
  3. Wakes when the cell is already busy.
Yövahtirisk-aware planner
a forecast + a limit
  1. Looks 10 hours ahead at the pessimistic forecast.
  2. Checks the layer that receives the users, every quarter-hour.
  3. Wakes before the rush, and doesn't flap.
Same electricity bill. 6× fewer congested quarter-hours.
05Every method on one chart
Energy and congestion are one trade-off.
Every sleeping radio saves energy and pushes its users onto the coverage layer. Sleep more, save more, until users feel it. So the question isn't "how much do you save" but how much you save for the congestion you cause. Up and to the left is better.
Yövahti's margin is a dial along that curve: bold saves 18.1 %, careful saves 14.1 % with almost no congestion. Every point beats static rules and the O-RAN SC rApp logic.
Validation weeks, 749 real sectors, same forecasts and simulator for every method. Oracle = perfect foresight, the ceiling.

09NetCrash

11Night Shift · AI-RAN
Every AI-RAN site gets a GPU. At 3 a.m. it's idle.
The same forecast that decides when radios sleep decides how much of the base station's GPU can be lent to AI work, and only to tenants with a signed test result. The radio keeps what the pessimistic forecast says it needs; a watchdog evicts tenants the moment load rises.
